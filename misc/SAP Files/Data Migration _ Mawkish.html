What actually moves

Your data from today, not your history.

A new SAP system starts on a clean date. You bring over what you need to keep working the next morning: the records you use, what's still open, and the balances that carry forward. Years of closed transactions stay where they are, but you can still look them up.

Master data

Who and what you deal with

  • Customers and suppliers, with bank and tax details
  • Products, bills of material and routings
  • Fixed assets
  • Prices, contracts and conditions
Open items

Work still in progress

  • Unpaid customer and supplier invoices
  • Open sales and purchase orders
  • Open projects and service orders
  • Contracts still running
Balances

What carries forward

  • General ledger balances
  • Stock quantities and values
  • Asset values and depreciation to date
  • Bank balances for reconciliation
Usually stays behind

Kept, not migrated

  • Closed transactions from past years
  • Duplicate and inactive records
  • Workarounds the new process removes

We keep history readable, in the old system in read-only mode or in an archive or reporting store.

Order matters

Load in the right sequence, or nothing reconciles.

An open invoice can't load before its customer exists, and a stock balance can't load before its product. SAP's migration objects depend on each other, so we plan the loads as a sequence. The general ledger goes last, because it has to agree with everything loaded before it.

SAP's own tools

The migration cockpit is built in, and it checks every record.

SAP Cloud ERP includes the migration cockpit, the Migrate Your Data app, at no extra cost. It loads data through the same checks as the business apps, so a record that wouldn't save on screen won't load either. There are three ways to feed it, and we pick per object.

How we rehearse

Three practice loads before the one that counts.

We never do a migration for the first time on go-live weekend. Each practice load, called a mock load, runs the full process on a test system with your real data. Errors fall with every round, and by the final rehearsal we know exactly how long cutover takes.

Illustrative values. Your error rates depend on the source system and how much cleaning happens up front.

Clean before you load

Fix the data where it lives, before it moves.

Most migration problems are data problems: the same customer entered three times, suppliers without bank details, products without weights. We profile your source data early and give each problem to the person in your business who owns it.

In Sri Lanka this now matters more than ever. With RAMIS e-invoicing, invoices go to the Inland Revenue Department in real time, so a missing or wrong customer TIN is a rejected invoice on day one, not a correction at month-end.

Reconcile and sign off

Every total checked, by the people who own it.

Loading data is half the job. The other half is proving it arrived complete and correct. After each load we compare the new system with the old one, and the business owner of each area signs it off.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

Do we lose our history?

No. History isn't loaded into the new system, but you can still see it. The usual options are to keep the old system available in read-only mode for a period, or to move history into an archive or a reporting store. We agree the option and how long you keep it, with your auditors' requirements in mind.

We aren't on SAP today. Can you still migrate us?

Yes. Most of our sources are non-SAP systems, such as IFS, Microsoft Dynamics, Oracle, Sage, Tally or custom-built software. We extract the data, map it to SAP's templates and load it with the migration cockpit. Only moves from SAP ECC can also use the direct transfer option.

Who cleans the data, you or us?

Both. We profile the data, find the problems and build the rules and mappings. Your people decide what's correct, because they know which of three customer records is the real one. Fixing data at the source is always cheaper than fixing it after go-live.

How long is the business frozen at cutover?

Usually a weekend, sometimes a little longer for large volumes. The final mock load is timed, so the cutover plan is based on a measured run, not an estimate. We agree beforehand which transactions stop, and when.

Can we load data again later, for example after an acquisition?

Yes. The migration cockpit stays available after go-live, so a new company or a new business can be loaded the same way.

How is personal data handled?

Migration files contain personal data such as employee and customer contacts. We agree secure storage and transfer, limit access to named people, and delete working files when the project closes. The cockpit also deletes its migration data after a retention period you choose.
