Cloud ERP Implementation

An SAP project that runs like a plan, not a gamble.

Implementing SAP Cloud ERP is a defined sequence of decisions, builds and rehearsals. Getting them in the right order is what separates a calm go-live from a crisis. A typical project

Seven months from kick-off to go-live.

A mid-sized company implementing finance, sales, procurement and inventory in one country, as a single phase. Bigger scope, more countries or heavy integration make it longer, and a phased rollout spreads it out. The shape stays the same.

Illustrative plan. SAP upgrades the system twice a year. If an upgrade falls inside the project, we plan around it rather than letting it surprise us: about a month of reduced change while new features are assessed and regression tests run.

The systems

Four systems, one direction of travel.

You start with a starter system preloaded with SAP's best practices and sample data. That's where the workshops happen. After the workshops, SAP provides your own development, test and production systems. Every change is built in development, proven in test and moved to production through controlled transports. Nobody configures production directly.

The starter system expires a year after contract start, so we plan the workshops early in the project.

What we tell every customer upfront

Six things you should hear before you sign.

None of these are problems if you plan for them. All of them become problems if nobody mentions them.

1

The starter system has a 12-month clock

It expires a year after contract start, so workshops need to happen early. A slow start costs you time you can't get back.

2

SAP upgrades don't wait for your project

If a release lands mid-project, expect about a month of reduced change while it's tested. We build it into the plan from day one.

3

A few early choices are permanent

Accounting standards, fiscal year, group currency and confirmed organisation units can't be changed later without rebuilding.

4

Role design takes weeks, not days

SAP's demo roles are too broad for security and for licensing. Proper roles need your data owners' input, and we start early.

5

Custom integrations are yours to keep

SAP maintains its prebuilt integrations through upgrades. Custom ones need an owner after go-live, either your team or ours.

6

Your experts' time is the critical path

If the people who know the processes can't attend workshops and testing, the project waits. Leadership needs to make it their priority.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

How long will our implementation take?

For a mid-sized company with standard scope in one country, typically six to eight months. More countries, complex manufacturing, many integrations or large data volumes extend it. We give you a firm plan after the readiness assessment and workshops.

Can we go live in phases?

Yes, and many companies do. The usual pattern is finance, sales and procurement first, then manufacturing, warehouse or more countries in later phases. SAP offers a parallel project line, so later phases can be built without disturbing the live system.

What happens to our old system?

It stays read-only after go-live for reference and audit. We migrate master data, open items and balances, and you decide how much history to bring across. Often the answer is less than you'd expect, because older data is still searchable in the old system.

Who owns the system after go-live?

You do. SAP runs the infrastructure and upgrades. Your team, or Mawkish through our support service, looks after the application: users, roles, changes, integrations and testing each release.

Will our users need a lot of training?

Less than with older ERPs. SAP's apps are built around specific tasks and include in-app help and guided simulations. We train key users first, who then support their colleagues, with role-based sessions before go-live.
