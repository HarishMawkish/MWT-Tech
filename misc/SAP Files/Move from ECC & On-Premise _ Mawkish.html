Move from ECC & S/4HANA On-Premise

Choose your route to the cloud before the deadline chooses it for you.

Whether you're on SAP ECC or an older on-premise SAP S/4HANA release, there are four ways into SAP's cloud ERP. Each keeps something different and costs something different. We help you pick the right one, prepare your system for it, and run the move with rehearsed, measured downtime.

The deadlines

Four options for an ECC system, and what each one costs you.

SAP's dates are fixed, and the window for the cheapest options is already narrowing. Before choosing a route, it helps to see every option on the table, including the ones we wouldn't recommend.

Option 1

Stay, pay extended maintenance

Keep ECC on SAP support until the end of 2030.

  • Buys three years
  • Two-point premium on your maintenance fee
  • Only the last three enhancement packages qualify
  • Pushes the project into the busiest years for SAP skills
Option 2

Switch to third-party support

Leave SAP support for an independent provider.

  • Lower annual fee
  • No SAP legal, tax or security updates
  • Reinstatement fees if you return to SAP
  • The move still has to happen eventually
Option 3

RISE transition option

Run ECC in SAP's private cloud with support until 2033, as a bridge.

  • Three more years for very complex estates
  • Only with a RISE contract, on SAP HANA by end of 2030
  • Price uplift for commitments made after 2025
  • Essential fixes only, no new features
Option 4 · Our advice for most

Plan the move now

Choose a route and start the preparation work this year.

  • Your timing, not SAP's deadline
  • Preparation done while ECC is stable
  • Partner and SAP capacity still available
  • Benefits start years earlier

Dates and conditions as published by SAP and reported by SAPinsider. The transition option also requires a minimum system size and SAP's top support plan. Confirm current terms for your contract.

Already on S/4HANA?

Your release has a date too.

Moving from ECC to S/4HANA on-premise wasn't the end of the road. Each on-premise release has its own maintenance end. Releases before 2023 got five years of mainstream maintenance. From 2023, SAP ships a release every two years, each with seven years of maintenance.

If your release is close to its end, the choice is another on-premise upgrade project, or moving to SAP Cloud ERP Private, where SAP runs the upgrades.

Selective data transition

The middle path, in four techniques.

Between converting everything and starting from scratch, there's a set of specialist techniques for choosing exactly what moves. They suit groups that want to restructure during the move, for example merging company codes, carving out a business, or going live country by country.

Shell conversion

Your system's configuration and custom code are converted without the business data. Selected data is then loaded into the clean shell.

Use when: your configuration is good but you want to leave old data and history behind.

Mix and match

A new S/4HANA system is installed, and only the configuration and code you choose are carried across. Everything else is rebuilt on standard.

Use when: some areas work well and others need a redesign.

Client transfer

A complete client, with its configuration, master and transaction data, moves into the new system. Several source systems can be merged.

Use when: you're consolidating several SAP systems into one.

Company code transfer

One company code at a time moves across with its data, while the rest of the business stays on ECC until its turn.

Use when: you want phased go-lives by country or business unit.

Selective data transition uses specialist tools from SAP and certified partners. It needs more planning than a conversion, but gives you control that neither of the other routes can.

Start now

Six things you can do this quarter, whatever route you pick.

The work that makes migrations late is mostly preparation, and most of it can start before you've signed anything. Every item below makes the move shorter, cheaper and less risky.

Run SAP's Readiness Check

A free SAP analysis of your system: which SAP changes affect you, how much custom code you use, and how large your data really is.

Why now: it turns opinions into numbers for your business case.

Convert customers and suppliers

S/4HANA replaces separate customer and supplier records with a single business partner. The conversion can be done, and tested, in ECC today.

Why now: it's often the longest data task, and it doesn't need the new system.

Archive old data

Archive closed years of documents you no longer need online. A smaller database means a shorter conversion weekend and lower running costs.

Why now: archiving has its own lead time and approvals.

Retire unused custom code

Usage statistics show which programs nobody has run in a year. Deleting them now means nothing to adapt, test or migrate later.

Why now: every object removed is one less to fix.

Clean up finance

S/4HANA brings everything into one financial journal. Asset accounting, ledgers and reconciliation issues need to be resolved first.

Why now: finance checks are the most common conversion blocker.

Cleanse master data

Duplicate customers, blocked suppliers, unused materials and missing tax numbers. Fix them once at the source.

Why now: it pays off on every route, including a fresh start.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

When exactly does SAP stop supporting ECC?

Mainstream maintenance ends at the end of 2027. Extended maintenance runs from 2028 to the end of 2030 at a two-point premium, and only for the last three enhancement packages. After that, the only SAP-supported option is the RISE transition option to 2033, which needs a RISE contract and an SAP HANA system moved into SAP's private cloud by the end of 2030.

Should we wait for the 2033 option?

Only if your landscape is genuinely too complex to move by 2030. It provides essential fixes but no new features, the price rises for later commitments, and the project still has to happen. For most mid-sized companies, it costs more than it saves.

We're already on S/4HANA on-premise. Do we need to move?

Check your release. Releases before 2023 had five years of mainstream maintenance, and several have ended or end soon. From 2023, each release gets seven years. Moving an existing S/4HANA system to SAP Cloud ERP Private is the shortest route on this page and stops the cycle of upgrade projects.

Our ECC runs on Oracle or SQL Server. Is that a problem?

No, but it adds a database migration to SAP HANA. SAP's conversion tools handle the database migration and the software conversion in one run. The database size after archiving is what drives the downtime.

What happens to our history if we choose a fresh start?

Open items, balances and master data move across. Older history usually stays in a read-only copy of the old system or an archive for audit and reference. Selective data transition can bring a defined number of years of history into the new system if you need it.

How long does a conversion take?

Typically 12 to 24 months from assessment to go-live, depending on system size, custom code, data quality and integrations. Preparation work done in advance shortens it.

Can we change processes during a conversion?

Keep it limited. The safest approach is to convert first, then improve processes in planned waves after go-live. If you need to restructure during the move, for example by merging company codes, selective data transition is the better route.
