Built for Sri Lanka.

Sri Lanka's tax rules have changed more in the last three years than in the decade before. Here's what your SAP system has to handle today, and how we make it do so.

The honest starting point

SAP doesn't ship a Sri Lanka version. That's where we come in.

SAP provides ready-made local versions of its cloud ERP for about 60 countries, with tax, legal reporting and payment formats maintained by SAP. Sri Lanka isn't one of them, and neither are Bangladesh, Pakistan or the Maldives.

SAP Cloud ERP still runs in Sri Lanka. It's set up from a template country's settings, with a Sri Lanka layer built on top. But SAP doesn't keep that layer up to date when the rules change. Whoever builds it has to maintain it. So you need a partner who reads every IRD notice, not one who learns about them at year-end.

Ready for every country you operate in.

Many of our clients start in Sri Lanka and grow into India, the Middle East and beyond. The method that makes Sri Lanka work is the same one we use to roll SAP out across borders.

The global template

Design once. Localise each country. Roll out in waves.

A global template is your group's way of working in SAP: one chart of accounts, one set of processes, one set of reports. Each country adds only what its law requires, such as local tax, invoicing, banking and statutory reports. Nothing else changes from one country to the next.

The result is a group that closes the books the same way everywhere, shares data without translation, and opens a new country in weeks instead of months.

Country coverage

Two kinds of country, one approach.

In about 60 countries, SAP maintains the local version itself, and we configure and connect it. Everywhere else, we build the local layer the way we do for Sri Lanka. Either way, the global template stays the same.

The rollout playbook

Each new country is faster than the last.

After the first country, a rollout reuses the template, the test library and the training material. The work narrows to what's actually local. SAP's parallel project line lets us build the next country without disturbing the ones already live.

Step 1

Country assessment

Legal entities, tax registrations, e-invoicing, banking, payroll and statutory reports.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

Can SAP Cloud ERP really run a Sri Lankan company?

Yes. SAP doesn't provide a ready-made Sri Lanka version, but the system is configured from a template country and a Sri Lanka layer is added for VAT, SSCL, withholding tax, RAMIS integration, invoice formats and bank files. The difference is who maintains that layer: in Sri Lanka, it's your partner, not SAP.

What happens when the IRD changes a rate or a rule?

We track IRD notices, circulars and budget changes, assess the impact on your system, and apply the change before it takes effect. Most rate changes are configuration. Changes to schedules or the RAMIS API may need a small development, which we plan with you.

Do we have to connect to RAMIS?

The IRD is rolling out the VAT Web API in phases, starting with export-oriented businesses and expecting full integration by the end of 2026. Records can also be uploaded by CSV, but an ERP integration is the only practical option at volume. We build it as part of the project.

We export in US dollars. Can SAP handle LKR and USD?

Yes. SAP keeps several currencies on every posting: transaction, company and group currency. We decide early which currency each company reports in, because group currency is one of the settings that can't be changed later.

Do you only work in Sri Lanka?

No. We're based in Colombo and serve clients in Sri Lanka, India, the UAE and beyond. Many projects start with a Sri Lankan head office and roll out to subsidiaries abroad. The global template approach is designed for exactly that.

How long does it take to add a country?

Once the template is live, a new country in SAP's local-version list typically takes a few months, depending on scope and integrations. Countries that need a local layer built take longer the first time, and less after that.
