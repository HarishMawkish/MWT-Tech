## USAGE RULES — read before putting any of this on the website

1. **Case-study result percentages, module lists and integrations are indicative, not verified.**
   The deck's own notes say so, slide after slide: the result bands, the "MODULES DEPLOYED"
   lists and the integration rows are, in the deck's words, "assumptions approved by Nafaz and
   not yet confirmed" with the client. Never publish any of them as fact without the user's
   explicit confirmation. The same applies to the `99.7%` figure that appears on seven case
   studies (CIC, Capital Maharaja, CBL, Suwanda, Luminous, RR Kabel, Lohia): the notes state it
   is **SAP's standard production availability SLA** for S/4HANA
   Cloud Private Edition under RISE, **not a measured figure for that client**.

2. **Vendor and partner facts must be re-checked on official pages before they go on the
   website.** Figures in this deck about SAP, Salesforce, Databricks, MiHCM, Samishti, Deloitte
   and Odoo were correct for a November 2026 pitch deck and are not a substitute for reading
   the vendor's own page. The deck notes themselves say this twice — "Product facts come from
   public sources; confirm before quoting them" (MiHCM) and "Figures come from Samishti's
   website and partner listings; confirm with Saurab before quoting them outside the room".
   See the project standard in the session memory on verified facts only.

3. **Do not claim a Databricks project track record.** The deck is explicit: "Mawkish is a
   Databricks partner; we do not yet have Databricks projects to reference, so position this as
   capability and access, not track record." Databricks is capability and access only.

4. **Do not claim a formal Anthropic partnership beyond the deck's wording.** The deck says only
   "Anthropic — AI implementation — Claude-powered agents and workflows" and, under managed
   services, "AI roadmap with SAP Business AI, Joule and Anthropic". It never says partner,
   reseller or certified. Do not upgrade that language.

5. **Client names and logos are not automatically publishable.** The deck records that for John
   Keells Restaurants "the restaurant brand is not named on this slide and its logo is not
   used", and that several logos were supplied by Nafaz for internal deck use. Treat logo and
   naming rights as unresolved. In particular, the client wall on slide 4 carries a **Sun
   Siyam** logo that is named nowhere in the deck's text or notes — do not use that name or
   logo until the user says what the engagement is.

6. **The removed slide.** The deck originally had 42 slides. The "IN OUR CLIENT'S WORDS · CIC
   HOLDINGS PLC" pull-quote slide (Dhammika Jayasundara, General Manager, Information
   Technology) was removed on 2026-11-03 at the owner's request. **Do not reinstate that quote
   or attribute it anywhere.** CIC Holdings remains a case study in the deck; only the quote

## Operating philosophy and commitments

Three slides carry the cultural argument, and they are the most distinctive content in the deck.

### "We build the back of the cabinet"

Framed with a Steve Jobs quotation from 1985 — *"You're not going to use a piece of plywood on
the back, even though it faces the wall and nobody will ever see it."* The slide's claim: **"The
details nobody sees are the ones that decide whether a project succeeds. We look after every one
of them."**

The accompanying diagram, **"One deal, many parties"**, sets out three roles across the phases
presales → implementation → post go-live support:

- **Software principal** (SAP, Salesforce, AWS) — provides the platform, licences and product
  support.
- **Implementation partner** (specialist delivery team) — configures, builds and deploys the
  solution.
- **Mawkish — your accountable partner** — owns quality across every phase and every party, and
  checks every handover.

The landing line: *"Whoever does the work, Mawkish checks it, owns it and answers for it, from
the first presales meeting to long after go-live."* The notes add the argument that most
problems in a project hide in the gaps between those parties, and owning those gaps is Mawkish's
role.

### What a contract award means to us

A chain-of-trust slide: the **champion** who put the name forward first, the **evaluation team**
that tested team, approach and commercials, **management** who backed the recommendation, and
**leadership and the board** who awarded the contract. *"Each of them put their name behind us.
We protect that trust as if it were our own."*

### Our commitment to you — "Always on the client's side of the table"

Four commitments, stated plainly:

1. **Client first, every time** — "We act in your best interest, whichever principal or partner
   is involved, **including our own**."
2. **Independent scrutiny** — "We review every proposal, change request and invoice from
   principals and partners before it reaches you."
3. **Fair commercials** — "No licences you do not need, no inflated scope and no change orders
   that should have been in the plan."
4. **We stand with you** — "When something goes wrong, we escalate on your behalf and stay until
   it is resolved."

Closing line: *"Our partners deliver the technology. Our loyalty stays with you."*

## The lifecycle — what Mawkish does

Five stages, under the heading "What Mawkish does · Bringing Our A Game":

1. **Presales — advise and select.** Expert engagement from our own consultants; representing
   your interest with every vendor; commercial advisory for the best ROI; principal coordination
   on your behalf; best-fit deployment partner alignment.
2. **Before kickoff — scope and contract.** Scope finalized by our consulting team; one contract
   and one commercial owner; formal partnerships with tier-one principals; clear plan, milestones
   and governance.
3. **Implementation — deliver and govern.** Local project office run by experienced PMs; build by
   our in-house team or specialist partners; data migration, testing and QA oversight; structured
   cutover and go-live.
4. **Go-live and beyond — adopt and enable.** Role-based user training; change management support;
   adoption acceleration; knowledge transfer to your team.
5. **Managed services — run and improve.** SAP S/4HANA annual support; L1, L2 and L3 support; AWS
   technical support from our own team; AI roadmap with SAP Business AI, Joule and Anthropic;
   governance and health checks.

**Throughout, Mawkish owns:** the client relationship; the contract and commercials; the local
project office; scope finalization; implementation governance; delivery accountability. This
six-item ownership list is repeated verbatim on the partners slide, and is the single most
load-bearing claim in the deck.

## Messaging lines and tone of voice

The deck's voice is **plain, concrete and self-effacing about its own role**. It sells
accountability rather than technology, and it is unusually willing to name what it does *not* do
(Samishti implements; Swyftflo implements; MiHCM implements). Sentences are short. Numbers are
always paired with what they mean for the client. British spelling is mixed with American
("organizations", "organisation" both appear).

value creation and continuous improvement).

## Known deck inconsistencies

Listed for the user to decide on. **Nothing here has been "fixed" in the deck.**

1. **RISE with SAP count: 32 vs 23.** The case-study divider slide shows **"32 — 46% of
   delivery — RISE WITH SAP"**, but its own speaker notes say **"23 RISE with SAP"**. Only 23 is
   arithmetically consistent: 23+12+6+3+3+2+1 = **50**, matching the headline, and 23/50 = **46%**,
   matching the printed share. With 32 the counts total **59**, and 32/50 would be 64%, not 46%.
   The index slide repeats the wrong figure in **"8 case studies among 32"**. **One number needs
   changing in two places — and 23 is the one the rest of the deck supports.** Confirmed from
   the render: the slide's proportional stacked bar is **drawn to 46%**, i.e. to 23, so the
   chart, the percentages, the column sum and the notes all agree and only the numeral is
   wrong — see the measurements in `company-profile-visuals.md`.

2. **Contents slide page ranges are now off by one at the end.** Part 06 reads **"SLIDES
   38–42"**; after the slide-40 removal the deck ends at 41, so it should read **38–41**. Parts
   01–05 (3–9, 10–11, 12–16, 17–21, 22–37) are still correct.

3. **Contents slide says "client voices", plural.** With the CIC pull-quote slide removed there
   is now exactly one client-voice slide (Eswaran Brothers).

4. **Email typo on the closing slide:** "info@mawkish technologies.com" has a stray space. The
   speaker notes and slide 1 both have it correct as `info@mawkishtechnologies.com`.

5. **Eswaran Brothers export markets: 60+ vs 57+.** The case-study slide says **60+ export

   split — "exports to 60+ countries per the company (Daily FT says 57+)" — but the deck prints
   both without explanation.

6. **Eswaran Brothers: founded 1963 vs "8 decades in tea".** The case-study slide gives **1963**
